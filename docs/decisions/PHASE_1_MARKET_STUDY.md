# Phase 1 Market Study

Last updated: March 16, 2026

## Purpose

This document reviews comparable products and design patterns relevant to CanStack.

The goal is not to copy competitors.
The goal is to understand:

- what kinds of products already exist
- how they position themselves
- what they emphasize in the UI
- what lessons are relevant for CanStack

## Scope

Comparables reviewed:

- Catch
- QuickBooks Solopreneur
- Wagepoint
- Wave
- Collective
- Gusto

These are not perfect one-to-one competitors.
They are adjacent comparables across:

- self-employed benefits
- solopreneur finance
- payroll/compliance
- contractor administration

## Executive Summary

### 1. There is no dominant single product that matches CanStack exactly

Verdict: [VERIFIED]

Reasoning:

- Catch focuses on portable benefits and tax withholding for freelancers.
- QuickBooks Solopreneur focuses on one-person business finances and tax readiness.
- Wagepoint focuses on Canadian payroll and compliance for small businesses.
- Wave focuses on accounting, invoicing, payments, and payroll.
- Collective focuses on bookkeeping/accounting plus back-office support for solopreneurs.
- Gusto focuses on payroll, contractor payments, benefits, and compliance automation for employers and growing teams.

## What The Market Suggests For CanStack

## 1. CanStack should not present benefits and compliance as equal homepage categories

Verdict: [PLAUSIBLE]

Reasoning:

- The comparable products usually lead with one dominant user need:
  - benefits
  - payroll
  - bookkeeping
  - tax readiness
- They may support adjacent tasks, but they do not ask the user to interpret a broad platform taxonomy immediately.

What would confirm it further:

- 5-second tests
- click behavior on the current homepage
- user interviews

## 2. The best top-level framing for CanStack is "self-employed operator" or "one-person business" rather than generic "contractor"

Verdict: [PLAUSIBLE]

Reasoning:

- QuickBooks Solopreneur and Collective both use role-specific framing.
- Wagepoint uses "small business owner" and role-based segmentation.
- Generic "contractor" is broader and can mean tradesperson, freelancer, incorporated consultant, or subcontractor.

What would confirm it further:

- user feedback on headline comprehension
- homepage scoring pass

## 3. The strongest CanStack pattern is a staged operator journey

Verdict: [VERIFIED]

Reasoning:

- Gusto explicitly supports moving from contractor administration to broader employer operations.
- Catch and QuickBooks Solopreneur each lead with a specific stage of self-employed admin burden.
- This supports CanStack's current strategy of one product with stages rather than two unrelated products.

Sources:

- [Gusto contractor payroll](https://gusto.com/es/product/solutions/size/contractor)
- [Catch](https://s.catch.co/lic/)
- [QuickBooks Solopreneur](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/press-releases/intuit-introduces-quickbooks-solopreneur/)

## 4. Trust cues matter more than visual novelty in this category

Verdict: [VERIFIED]

Reasoning:

- Wagepoint foregrounds Canadian compliance, pricing transparency, support, and security.
- Collective foregrounds disclaimers and support.
- Gusto foregrounds support, tax automation, and growth readiness.

Sources:

- [Wagepoint](https://www.wagepoint.com/)
- [Collective](https://www.collective.com/bookkeeping-and-accounting)
- [Gusto contractor payroll](https://gusto.com/es/product/solutions/size/contractor)

## Concrete Phase 1 Implications

### Recommendation 1

Verdict: [VERIFIED]

- Evaluate whether the homepage should lead with:
  - `Run your self-employed business with confidence`
  - or `Plan your business and stay ahead of deadlines`

Reasoning:

- Comparable products lead with the user problem, not internal module labels.

### Recommendation 2

Verdict: [VERIFIED]

- Evaluate whether the user should choose a stage first:
  - getting set up
  - running solo
  - managing payroll/subcontractors

Reasoning:

- Wagepoint uses role segmentation.
- Gusto uses growth-stage framing.

### Recommendation 3

Verdict: [VERIFIED]

- Keep the two-workspace model internally, but consider hiding it behind a stronger role or stage entry model on the homepage.

Reasoning:

- This preserves product structure without forcing the user to understand the platform taxonomy on first load.

### Recommendation 4

Verdict: [VERIFIED]

- Strengthen explicit trust cues on the homepage:
  - Canadian-specific
  - hardcoded public rules
  - estimate disclaimers
  - secure handling of profile data

Reasoning:

- The strongest adjacent products consistently foreground trust and relief from admin risk.
