## Revenue Stream Audit: Can You Make Money Without a License?


***

> **Claim:** "Selling a \$29/mo SaaS subscription for a compliance calendar requires a license"
> **Reasoning:** Selling software as a service (deadline tracking, penalty calculators, tax bracket tools) is a general commercial activity in Canada. It is not financial advice, insurance distribution, or investment dealing. No Canadian statute regulates selling calendar/calculator software. CRA information tools (e.g., H\&R Block's free tax calculator) operate without licensing. The only license requirement would trigger if you began giving personalized advice.
> **Verdict:** [VERIFIED — SOURCE: No insurance or securities license required to sell SaaS tools. Business license required per province (Ontario Business Registry, ~\$60/yr). GST/HST registration required once revenue >\$30k. SOURCE: https://www.godaddy.com/resources/ca/launch/who-needs-a-business-license-under-canadian-law + https://www.srjca.com/blog/is-saas-software-as-a-service-taxable-in-canada/][^1][^2]
>
> **Gap to fill:** Register a business (sole prop or corporation) with Ontario Business Registry. Register for GST/HST with CRA once you hit \$30k revenue (or earlier voluntarily). Cost: ~\$60–500 depending on structure.

***

> **Claim:** "You can earn Zensurance affiliate commissions (\$50 CPA) without an insurance license"
> **Reasoning:** Zensurance's affiliate program is live on Fintel Connect and explicitly open to non-licensed publishers. This is consistent with how most property/casualty insurers run CPA marketing programs globally — the affiliate sends traffic, the licensed insurer closes the transaction. The affiliate does not advise, quote, or bind coverage.
> **Verdict:** [VERIFIED — SOURCE: https://www.fintelconnect.com/brands/directory/zensurance-affiliate-program/][^3]
>
> **Gap to fill:** Register at Fintel Connect, apply for the Zensurance program, get approved. Takes 1–5 business days. No license needed. Quebec excluded — do not run affiliate links for Quebec users.

***

> **Claim:** "You can earn commissions from health/disability insurance referrals without a license"
> **Reasoning:** Ontario's Insurance Act and FSRA rules are explicit: receiving compensation for referring clients to insurance products is regulated activity if it constitutes "soliciting" or "negotiating" insurance. The Ontario Insurance Act s.1 definition of "insurance agent" includes anyone who "for compensation, acts or aids" in placing insurance. RIBO prohibits licensed brokers from paying referral fees to unlicensed parties — which indirectly blocks your revenue stream.
> **Verdict:** [MISLEADING — CORRECTION: You cannot legally receive commissions from Ontario health/disability insurance referrals without an insurance license or being a party to an approved non-licensed affiliate program. The operative constraint is on the insurer/broker paying you, not on you directly. Without a documented program (like Zensurance/Fintel Connect), there is no legal path to be paid for health insurance referrals in Ontario without LLQP. SOURCE: FSRA TLE framework + RIBO Code of Conduct 2023.][^4][^5]
>
> **Gap to fill:** Either (a) get LLQP (~\$900, 6–8 weeks part-time) to unlock health/disability commissions, or (b) find a health insurer that runs a documented non-licensed affiliate program (none found in research to date). Option (a) is the only verified path.

***

> **Claim:** "You can charge users a subscription without needing any regulatory permission"
> **Reasoning:** Stripe subscriptions for SaaS tools are standard commercial activity. No Canadian financial regulator governs charging a monthly fee for software access. FSRA, OSC, and AMF regulate financial *products and advice*, not software subscriptions. The only obligations are: business registration, GST/HST collection, and PIPEDA compliance.
> **Verdict:** [VERIFIED — REASON: Consistent with how all Canadian SaaS businesses operate. No license required to charge \$29/mo for a compliance calendar. SOURCE: https://www.srjca.com/blog/is-saas-software-as-a-service-taxable-in-canada/][^1]
>
> **Gap to fill:** Collect and remit GST/HST on subscriptions (13% Ontario HST on digital services). Register voluntarily early — avoids back-remittance risk at \$30k threshold.

***

> **Claim:** "Collecting income range, province, and work type from users requires PIPEDA compliance"
> **Reasoning:** PIPEDA applies to any private-sector organization collecting personal information in the course of commercial activity. Income range is classified as sensitive financial information by the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada — requiring express (not implied) consent. Province and work type alone are lower sensitivity but still require consent. Collecting all three together (income + province + work type) builds a financial profile — this triggers stronger consent obligations.
> **Verdict:** [VERIFIED — SOURCE: https://complydog.com/blog/pipeda-compliance-guide-canadian-software-companies + OPC Guidelines on Meaningful Consent: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/collecting-personal-information/consent/gl_omc_201805/][^6][^7]
>
> **Gap to fill (3 concrete items):**
> 1. Write a Privacy Policy (use a Canadian-specific template, not a US GDPR template — they are different). Cost: \$300–500 with a lawyer, or use Termly/iubenda with Canada-specific settings.
> 2. Add a consent checkbox before the quiz that explicitly states what data is collected and why. Pre-checked boxes are illegal under PIPEDA.
> 3. Income range: store as a range string only (e.g., "\$50k–\$100k"), not an exact figure. Session-only unless user explicitly opts in to persistence.

***

> **Claim:** "You can use the FSRA/OSC Regulatory Sandbox to operate without a license temporarily"
> **Reasoning:** Canada's CSA Regulatory Sandbox exists and allows fintech firms to register and obtain exemptive relief from securities laws on a time-limited basis. FSRA also operates Test and Learn Environments (TLEs) under s.15.1 of the Insurance Act. However: (a) CanStack's compliance calendar product does not appear to require any securities or insurance license in the first place — it's a software tool, not financial product distribution. (b) The sandbox is for companies that need a license but want to test first — not for companies whose product doesn't require one. Applying to the sandbox when you don't need a license wastes time.
> **Verdict:** [MISLEADING — CORRECTION: The sandbox is irrelevant for CanStack's core SaaS product (compliance calendar, tax estimator, RRSP calculator). It would only be relevant if you wanted to offer investment advice or insurance distribution without being licensed. You don't need it. Don't apply. SOURCE: https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/innovation-office + https://oscinnovation.ca/about/partnerships][^8][^4]
