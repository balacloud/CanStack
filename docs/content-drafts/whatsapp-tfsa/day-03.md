Topic: The withdraw-then-recontribute trap, and the over-contribution penalty
Sources verified 2026-09-22:
- canada.ca — "If you over-contribute to a TFSA" (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/tax-free-savings-account/contributing/overcontribute.html)
- canada.ca — "If you owe tax on excess TFSA amounts" (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/tax-free-savings-account/owing-tax/excess.html)
- Verified exact mechanism: withdrawn room is NOT restored until January 1st of the following year. Over-contribution penalty is 1% per month, calculated on the HIGHEST excess amount reached during that month (not the final amount) — you must still file a TFSA return with CRA to report it, due by June 30 of the following year.
- Note (not included in the WhatsApp text, flagging for awareness): CRA can apply a separate, much harsher 100% "advantage tax" for deliberate/abusive over-contribution schemes — that's a distinct anti-avoidance rule, not the standard 1%/month penalty. Not relevant to the ordinary "forgot to check my room" mistake this post is about, so left out to avoid overstating the risk of an honest mistake.

WHATSAPP DRAFT (review before posting):

Yesterday: newcomers start earning TFSA room from day one, no waiting period. Today's the mistake that trips up even experienced investors — withdrawing money does NOT free up that room right away, only on January 1st of the next year. So if you're maxed out and pull $10,000 out for a down payment, you can't put that $10,000 back in until the new year — try to, and you're now over-contributing. That costs 1% per month on whatever your highest excess was that month, and you still have to file a TFSA return with CRA just to report it.
