Newcomer residency note verified against: canada.ca — "Newcomers to Canada and the CRA" (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/newcomers-canada-immigrants.html). Correction from an earlier draft: there is NO fixed 183-day waiting period for most newcomers. CRA determines tax residency by residential ties (a home, spouse/dependants, etc.) — for most people who move to Canada to actually live here, residency (and TFSA room accrual) starts their very first day. The 183-day "deemed resident" rule is a separate, narrower test that applies only to people who do NOT establish those residential ties (e.g. present in Canada a lot without settling) — it is not the general rule and should not be implied as one.

WHATSAPP DRAFT (review before posting):

Yesterday: a TFSA is an investing wrapper, not a savings account. Today's the number that actually matters — how much room do you have? Every Canadian 18+ gets new room every January 1st regardless of income, and for 2026 that's $7,000. If you turned 18 in 2009 or earlier and have never contributed, your total available room right now is $109,000 — yes, six figures, just from unused room stacking up every year. For newcomers: you start earning room from the day you actually become a tax resident, which for most people is their very first day living here once they've got real ties (a home, etc.) — no fixed waiting period, contrary to what you might've heard.
