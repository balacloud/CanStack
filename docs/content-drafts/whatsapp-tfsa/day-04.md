TFSA part 2 — what to hold, taxes, and what happens when you die.

1. What actually belongs in it: REITs get taxed harder than regular dividends outside a TFSA, since their payouts don't qualify for the dividend tax credit — so REITs benefit the most from being sheltered. Growth stocks are already taxed lightly outside (capital gains get a 50% break), so the TFSA helps them less, though it's still fully tax-free inside either way.

2. US withholding tax: any US dividend gets a 15% withholding tax taken off, even inside a TFSA — the tax treaty exemption only applies to RRSP, RRIF, or LIRA. Capital gains on US stocks are still 100% tax-free in a TFSA, just not the dividends.

3. What happens when you die: name your spouse as successor holder and the TFSA just continues under their name, no tax, nothing changes. Name anyone else instead (kids, etc.) as beneficiary, and they get the account's value tax-free at death, but anything it earns after that point until it's paid out becomes taxable to them.

4. Two things most people don't know: you can have multiple TFSA accounts across different banks and brokers — the limit is on total contributions, not per account. And day trading inside a TFSA is risky beyond just losing money: CRA can decide you're running a trading business and tax the entire account's income as a result.
